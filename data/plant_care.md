# Plant Care Knowledge Base (Delhi-NCR)

Each paragraph below is one "chunk" the agent can retrieve. This is general,
public plant-care knowledge — not tied to any product or company.

Overwatering is the single most common cause of houseplant death. Soggy soil
starves roots of air and leads to root rot, shown by soft yellow leaves, mushy
stems and a musty smell. The fix is to stop watering and let the top 2 inches of
soil dry out before watering again. Always use a pot with a drainage hole.

Yellow leaves have six common causes and two need opposite fixes. A single old
bottom leaf going yellow is normal ageing — just snip it. Soft yellow leaves with
soggy soil mean overwatering (water less). Yellow with crispy brown edges and
bone-dry soil means thirst (water more). Blotchy yellow with green veins in
Delhi-NCR is usually hard water. Yellowing all over with stretching stems means
too little light. Tiny webs or dots under leaves mean pests.

Delhi-NCR tap water is hard, typically 400–700 TDS (high in calcium and minerals).
Over time these minerals build up in the soil and block roots from absorbing
nutrients, causing brown leaf tips and blotchy yellowing. The fix is to flush the
pot with RO or filtered water once a month for a minute and let it drain, or leave
tap water out overnight before using.

Spider mites thrive in Delhi-NCR's dry winter air (October onward). Early signs
are fine pale stippling on leaves, a dull dusty look, and fine webbing in the leaf
joints. The fix is to isolate the plant, wipe every leaf top and underside with
diluted neem oil weekly for three weeks, and raise humidity by misting.

Dust from Delhi-NCR air settles on leaves and blocks the light the plant needs to
photosynthesise. Wiping the leaves with a damp cloth (or a light mist and wipe)
every week keeps them clean and healthy. During pollution season, dust builds up
faster, so check leaves more often.

Low-light, hard-to-kill plants good for beginners and dim rooms include the snake
plant, ZZ plant, money plant (pothos), Aglaonema (Chinese evergreen) and lucky
bamboo. Snake and ZZ can go 2–3 weeks without water and tolerate low light,
making them ideal for offices and forgetful owners.

Watering frequency changes with the season in Delhi-NCR. In peak summer (May–June)
plants drink fast and may need water twice a week. During the monsoon and winter,
soil stays wet for days, so water far less — the same plant can go from twice a
week in June to once every 10–12 days in January. Always finger-test first.

Misting raises humidity only briefly, but it does clean dust off leaves and makes
the air around the plant less inviting to spider mites, which prefer dry air. It
is helpful in dry NCR winters but is not a substitute for proper watering.

Repot a plant when roots poke out of the drainage hole or growth stalls, usually
every 12–18 months, moving one pot size up. Do not jump to a very large pot — too
much soil stays wet and causes root rot. Use proper potting mix, not garden soil,
which compacts and brings pests indoors.

Light levels: 'bright indirect' means near a window but out of harsh direct sun;
'low light' means a few feet from a window or a north-facing room. Most popular
indoor plants prefer bright indirect light. Direct midday sun through NCR summer
glass can scorch leaves.
